Meteor75 Pro II O4 Wide: The Second Whoop After Your Analog Air65

Short version: the BETAFPV Meteor75 Pro II O4 Wide is a strong second quad, the step after an analog Air65: a 41.5 g, 1S, 80mm prop-guarded whoop with DJI's 159° O4 Wide camera that records 4K/60. It needs DJI Goggles 3, N3, 2 or Integra, and it isn't a first whoop. Learn on a 65mm analog like the Air65 first. Pack links go through /go/ affiliate redirects at no extra cost to you.

I've flown it: about three minutes per LAVA II 480 before I landed, with clear headroom left. I could have kept going. That's a normal session, not a drain test, and it roughly matches Oscar's 3:00–3:30 on a 480.

No DJI goggles? BETAFPV sells an analog Meteor75 Pro II too; see the analog section.

The step after analog, if your goggles fit

DJI lists Goggles 3, N3, 2 and Integra, and no third-party goggles, so compatible goggles are a requirement and the real cost. Once you can fly an Air65 cleanly, this is the upgrade that pays off. If you're starting from zero, the whoop video guide and Air vs Meteor cover the cheaper path: analog first, HDZero when the picture starts to matter.

No DJI goggles? Get the Meteor75 Pro II Analog

Flying analog goggles like the EV800D from First Tiny Whoop Setup? Get the BETAFPV Meteor75 Pro II Analog. Oscar calls it "essentially the same quad as the O4 version" (same frame, motors and props), with an analog VTX. BETAFPV lists 27.98 g (Oscar weighed 28.2 g), so it's about 13 g lighter and, per Oscar, punchier, minus 4K recording.

BETAFPV claims 10:30 on a LAVA II 580 and 11:30 on a 680; Oscar got about 4, 5 and 6 minutes on a 480, 580 and 680. Hard punch-outs triggered low-voltage warnings, so run mAh drawn in the OSD. It ships with one of several gyros, and BETAFPV's posted firmware is BMI270-only: check yours before you flash it.

O4 Wide is the reason to buy this one

The wider lens is the upgrade. DJI rates the O4 Wide at 159° against the standard O4's 117.6°, with the same 1/2-inch sensor, 4K/60 recording and 23 GB of built-in storage (no microSD). The 159° only applies when recording 4:3 with Camera FOV set to Wide and EIS off. The cost is weight: about 13.3 g for the Wide against 8.2 g for the standard O4.

What changed from the Meteor75 Pro O4

The whole Canopy II now floats on four shock-absorbing balls instead of soft-mounting only the camera.

Packs, and the flight time you'll actually get

It's 1S on BT2.0, and no battery ships in the box. BETAFPV claims 5:00 on a LAVA II 580 and 6:00 on a 680. Oscar Liang real-world: 3:00–3:30 on a 480, 4:00–4:30 on a 580, 4:30–5:00 on a 680. He prefers the 480 indoors and the 580 outside. Indoors, start with the LAVA II 480; the battery guide covers 75mm packs.

Before you flash it

BETAFPV lists a BMI270 gyro on Betaflight 2025.12.4 and posts its own hex and CLI file on its support page. Save a CLI diff first and flash that hex, not a guessed target.
