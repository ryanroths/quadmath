Whoop Video: Analog First, HDZero When You Care, DJI and Walksnail Later

Short version: a first whoop is analog. Move to HDZero when a fuzzy picture is the thing stopping you from flying tighter, and not before. DJI and Walksnail are for when the whoop has become a camera platform, which is a different hobby.

This page is only about video — camera, VTX, goggles. Sizes, radios and what to buy first are in the first whoop guide.

The decision, by what you fly

Analog: the default, and not a compromise

The Air65 analog build behind this site is 25.42 g all-up and cruises for 3:53 on a 300 mAh pack. A 65mm analog whoop is the lightest, longest-flying thing you can put in the air, and the picture is good enough to fly anything indoors. What analog gives you that no digital system does is honesty: when the signal gets weak you see static creep in and you back off. Digital hides it until it does not.

Goggles: any analog box goggle works. I started on an Eachine EV800D and moved to Fat Shark HDO2 much later. A 5.8 GHz monitor works. Spend the money on packs.

HDZero: the upgrade that costs flight time

HDZero is digital done the analog way — a sharp picture that breaks up gradually rather than freezing, with latency close enough to analog that racers use it. On a 65mm it is the only digital system light enough to make sense. It is also not free: on the same pack and props the HDZero build in the measured comparison flew about a third shorter than the analog one, and most of that cost is fixed — the VTX draws what it draws whether you are cruising or not.

Buy in when you have flown enough analog to know what a bad picture is costing you. Then buy the ecosystem, not a part: HDZero goggles or an HDZero module for the goggles you own, plus an HDZero whoop. Mixing systems does not work — an HDZero camera is invisible to analog goggles and the other way round.

DJI and Walksnail: later, and probably not on a 65mm

Both give the best image you can get from a quad. Both weigh more and draw more than a 65mm can carry without turning flight time into a rounding error, and their goggles are the most expensive part of any FPV setup. When the goal is footage — indoor tours, chasing cars, anything people will watch — a 75 or 85mm cinewhoop with one of these is the right tool. It is not a first whoop and it is not a freestyle whoop.

The rule

  • Goggles match the whoop. Decide the video system before you buy either.
  • Analog until the picture is the limit. It usually is not; the pilot is.
  • HDZero when it is. Budget for goggles plus quad, and for shorter packs.
  • DJI / Walksnail when the whoop exists to carry a camera.
